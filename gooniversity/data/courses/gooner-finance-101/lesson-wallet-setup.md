# Wallet Setup

> **Course:** Gooner Finance 101 · **Lesson:** 1 of 3

## Your Keys, Your Crypto

Right gooner, before you touch a single token, you need a wallet — and more importantly, you need to understand that your wallet doesn't actually hold crypto the way your bank holding pounds feels. It holds the private keys, which is a fancy way of saying the password that lets you spend your coins, and if you lose those keys, your money vanishes forever. No customer service, no password reset, no "oops I'll just log back in." This is why we don't trust exchange wallets for anything beyond quick trades — if FTX taught us anything, it's that someone else holding your keys is a one-way ticket to heartbreak. The gooner approach is self-custody, and the gateway drug is MetaMask.

## MetaMask Setup for Absolute Beginners

Download the MetaMask browser extension or mobile app first — and for the love of all that is holy, only get it from metamask.io or the official app stores, not from some dodgy ad that pops up when you're Googling at 2am. Install it, create a new wallet, and write that 12-word recovery phrase down on paper — not a screenshot, not a note on your phone, actual paper that you store in a drawer you can find again. Set a strong password, and enable two-factor authentication if your brain can handle the extra step. Once you're in, you'll see your wallet address — that's a string of letters and numbers that starts with "0x" and is basically your account number that you can share freely. The QR code next to it is how people send you money without you typing out a 42-character mess.

## Phantom for Solana Gang

If you're in the Solana camp instead of Ethereum, Phantom is your ride — it's the crypto equivalent of the Audi of wallets, smooth and fast with a UI that doesn't make you feel like you need a degree to operate it. The setup's almost identical, but that 12-word phrase is more important here because Solana wallets move faster and you can lose funds to a bad transaction in seconds. The gooner move is to keep a small amount on your hot wallet for daily swaps and park the bulk in a separate cold wallet — basically an old phone or hardware device that never connects to the internet except when you're actively moving large sums. And here's the thing nobody tells fresh gooners: don't announce your wallet address on social media, don't connect your wallet to sketchy websites just because they promise free tokens, and for crypto's sake don't DM anyone saying you're new and asking for help because you'll get hit with a dozen scam attempts by breakfast.
