# Engagement Loops

> **Course:** Community Mod Playbook · **Lesson:** 3 of 3

## The Daily Rhythm

Gooners are creatures of habit, so the trick to keeping the server buzzing is building predictable little dopamine hits throughout each day. The morning routine is straightforward — around 9am GMT the bot drops a daily challenge, something simple like "post your breakfast" or "share one song that's stuck in your head." These aren't fancy, but they get people into the habit of checking the server before their day proper kicks off. The afternoon slump around 3pm is prime time for quick polls — "who's winning the transfer rumours" or "worst kit design ever" — because those take five seconds to vote and five seconds to read the results. Evening is your big ticket item, whether that's a voice chat hangout, a trivia quiz, or just a dedicated shitposting hour where the rules get loosened and the memes flow free.

## Weekly Structure

The weekly cadence is where you turn casual lurkers into actual community members, and the gooner playbook is built around the familiar five-day work week. Monday morning kicks off with the weekly theme — what's the topic that'll tie the week together? Could be a movie, a game, a band, or even a stupid inside joke from last week that everyone's still quoting. Tuesday through Thursday you feed the discussion with daily prompts, trivia questions, and collaborative challenges that build on the theme. Friday is showcase day — let people post their takes, creations, or collections related to the week's topic and vote on favourites. The bot awards bonus points for participation, so you'll see the same gooners climbing the leaderboard by actually contributing instead of just lurking.

## The Retention Funnel

Here's the secret sauce that the veteran gooner mods know: you've got to guide people from passive scroll-through to active contributor in small, manageable steps. Stage one is pure consumption — let lurkers read and vote without the pressure to post. Stage two introduces low-stakes participation — emoji reactions, poll votes, quick one-liners in themed channels. Stage three is where you hook them for the long haul — they're starting threads, helping new members, and showing up to scheduled events week after week. The bot's progression system mirrors this perfectly: you earn enough points just from reading and reacting that you hit Copper rank without posting a single message, but the real perks kick in when you start contributing. A server that runs on these loops doesn't need a hundred new members every week — it just needs its existing gooners to show up and drag in one mate each.
