# Event Scheduling

> **Course:** Community Mod Playbook · **Lesson:** 2 of 3

## Picking a Time That Works

Right, the first rule of event scheduling is that you're never going to find a time that works for everyone, so stop trying. The gooner approach is to identify your core activity window — that's the two-to-three hour block where most of your regulars are naturally online, and you lock that in as your default slot. For European audiences that's usually 7-10pm GMT, for North American gooners it's 6-9pm EST, and the unholy hybrid servers run their main events at 11pm GMT to catch the east coast Americans and the late-night Europeans. Use the bot's polling feature to run a quick "what days work best" vote, but don't wait for consensus — pick a slot, announce it, and adjust after you've run it a few times. Consistency beats perfection every single time.

## Pre-Event Checklist

A smooth event is 90% preparation and 10% execution, so here's the gooner pre-flight checklist. Three days out, drop the announcement in the events channel with the date, time, and what you're actually doing — be specific, "game night" is too vague but "Among Us 4pm GMT with voice chat in Stage 2" tells people exactly what to expect. Ping your regulars, pin the announcement, and make sure the HeadMaster Bot has the event registered so it sends automatic reminders an hour and fifteen minutes out. The day before, double-check your voice channel capacity, confirm your screen share setup works, and test any external tools you're using — nothing kills a vibe faster than a 15-minute tech delay while everyone stares at a loading screen.

## Running the Show

On event day, the gooner mod shows up fifteen minutes early to open the channel, test permissions, and make sure the bot's countdown timer is live. Use stage channels for larger groups — it lets people queue to speak and keeps the chaos manageable. Keep the energy up during the event, call out new participants by name, and don't let one person dominate the conversation. After the event wraps, spend five minutes in the channel thanking everyone and announcing the next session — that's when the retention magic happens, because people remember being seen and appreciated. The bot's analytics will show you drop-off points and engagement peaks, so review that data and tweak the next event accordingly. A gooner who runs events people actually look forward to is worth their weight in server badges.
