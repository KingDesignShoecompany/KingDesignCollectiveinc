# Audience Growth

> **Course:** Stream Setup Academy · **Lesson:** 3 of 3

## The First 50 Followers

Getting those first 50 followers is a grind that every gooner has to claw through, and the dirty secret is that it's less about your content and more about showing up consistently. Pick a schedule you can actually keep — whether that's three times a week or every single day, just make it the same time so people know when to find you. Tell your friends and family to drop by, ask your existing Discord crew to lurk and chat, and actually engage with everyone who says hi instead of zoning out and playing the game on autopilot. The algorithm rewards streams with chat activity, so even if you're averaging two viewers, if those two people are having a conversation you're doing better than half the "just started streaming" channels that sit in dead silence for two hours.

## Cross-Platform Goonery

A lot of gooners put all their eggs in the Twitch basket and wonder why their channel stalls, but the real play is treating each platform as a different flavor of the same content. Clip your best moments — the clutch play, the funny reaction, the "wait what just happened" moment — and throw those clips on YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels with the same caption and a "full stream every night at 8." Each platform has its own peak hours, so don't just shotgun the same clip everywhere at 3am. Learn the rhythm: TikTok and Shorts eat up in the mornings, Instagram prefers lunchtime, and Twitter/X is a dumpster fire but still good for dropping a quick clip with hashtags. The goal isn't to go viral — it's to turn one platform's viewers into subscribers on your main channel.

## Retention Over Raw Numbers

Here's what separates the million-follower gooner legends from the accounts that peak at 5K and fade: retention. It's not about bringing in 500 new viewers for one stream and then watching them ghost you forever. It's about turning your 15 regulars into 30 regulars who each bring one friend, because that's how you build a real community. Run subscriber-only games, do community challenges, and remember people's names — when a viewer hears you call them by name they're way more likely to stick around for the next stream. Set up a Discord, run polls during your streams, and actually read and respond to everything in your notifications. Growth isn't a number, it's a habit, and habits are built one returning viewer at a time.
