# Budget Rig

> **Course:** Stream Setup Academy · **Lesson:** 1 of 3

## The $500 Goon Special

Look, we all start somewhere, and the last thing you need is to blow your entire bankroll on a rig that's gonna bottleneck your GPU anyway. The $500 Goon Special is a tried-and-tested build that gets you streaming at 1080p60 without crying yourself to sleep. Start with a used GTX 1660 Super or RTX 3060 — you can find these on Facebook Marketplace for a couple hundred bucks if you're patient. Pair it with an AMD Ryzen 5 3600 or Intel i5-10400F, both of which are dirt cheap on the secondhand market and handle a 6-core stream like they were born for it. 32GB of DDR4 isn't strictly necessary yet, but grab it anyway because OBS loves ram and you'll thank yourself when you're not hitting the swap file mid-broadcast.

## The Cheapest Decent Capture Card

If you're gaming on console and streaming from PC, the capture card is your gateway drug, and the EVGA XR1 Lite or the AverMedia Live Gamer Portable 2 are the gooner-approved budget kings. Expect to spend around $80-100 for a refurbished unit, just make sure it supports 1080p60 passthrough so you're not gaming at a crippled frame rate. A lot of gooners skip the capture card entirely and just stream straight from their console — that works for PS5/Series X but you lose a lot of the fancy OBS overlays. If you're serious about that channel branding, the capture card pays for itself in viewer retention.

## Peripherals That Don't Bust the Bank

The thing fresh gooners forget is that a stream is 50% presentation, and your mic and webcam are the face of your brand. Grab a decent USB mic — the Audio-Technica ATR2100x or Samson Q2U both run under $60 and sound a million times better than your headset mic. For the webcam, the Logitech C920 is basically the official gooner standard; you can find used ones for $30 and they'll still put you above 70% of streamers in the audio game. Spend the rest of your budget on a basic ring light or a clamp-on desk lamp with a daylight bulb, because good lighting fixes half the problems people assume are camera issues. Build it, test it, then get back to grinding — your audience grows with consistency, not with expensive gear.
